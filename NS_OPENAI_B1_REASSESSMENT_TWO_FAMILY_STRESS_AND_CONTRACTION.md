## 1. The Two-Family Stress Requirement

The OpenAI blowup mechanism strictly requires that the active Reynolds stress at scale $j$ generates a target deviatoric stress tensor $T \in \operatorname{Sym}_0(3)$ within the 2D stress cone $K$.
As proved in `CLM-037-B` and `NS_OPENAI_B1_R1_STRESS_CONE_TWO_WAVE_RECONSTRUCTION.md`, a single monochromatic plane wave $w = \operatorname{Re}(a e^{i k \cdot x})$ has rank-1 Reynolds stress $w \otimes w$ and cannot span a 2D cone.

Therefore, the autonomous entrance-state problem is fundamentally a **paired entrance problem**:
$$(W_+^\star, W_-^\star),$$
where $W_+^\star$ and $W_-^\star$ are two distinct helical wavepackets with distinct wavevectors $k_+^\star, k_-^\star$ and distinct polarization vectors $b_+, b_-$.

### 1.1 Coupling Matrix and Positivity
The 2D core stress components $T = (T_r, T_z)^T$ are generated by the linear combination:
$$T = y_+ C(b_+) + y_- C(b_-) = H y, \qquad H = \begin{pmatrix} C_{11}(b_+) & C_{11}(b_-) \\ C_{12}(b_+) & C_{12}(b_-) \end{pmatrix}.$$
Realization inside the open stress cone $\operatorname{int}(K)$ requires:
$$y = H^{-1} T > 0 \iff y_+ > 0 \quad \text{and} \quad y_- > 0.$$

For $H$ to be non-singular ($\det(H) \ne 0$), the two wave families must be linearly independent in stress space. In particular, their wavevectors $k_+^\star$ and $k_-^\star$ cannot be parallel or anti-parallel:
$$k_+^\star \nparallel k_-^\star.$$

## 2. Geometric Obstruction: Compressive Subspace Deficit

Now consider the backward evolution of both wavevectors $k_+^\star$ and $k_-^\star$ under the **same** autonomous background strain tensor $S(t) \in \mathfrak{sl}(3, \mathbb{R})$.

Recall that for a wavevector to de-shear backward with bounded viscous cost ($\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{back}} \le \mathcal{O}(1)$), it must lie entirely within the **backward-compressive eigenspace** of the strain tensor:
$$\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{comp}} = \left\{ v \in \mathbb{R}^3 : S(t) v = \lambda v \text{ with } \lambda < 0 \text{ (forward)} \right\}.$$

### 2.1 Spectral Dimension of Incompressible Strain
By incompressibility, the eigenvalues of $S(t)$ satisfy $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 0$.
The possible eigenvalue configurations for non-zero strain are:
1. **Axisymmetric Compressive (1D Compressive Axis)**:
   $$\lambda_1 = -2\sigma < 0, \qquad \lambda_2 = \sigma > 0, \qquad \lambda_3 = \sigma > 0 \quad (\sigma > 0).$$
   Here $\dim(\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{comp}}) = 1$.
2. **Axisymmetric Extensional (2D Compressive Subspace)**:
   $$\lambda_1 = -\sigma < 0, \qquad \lambda_2 = -\sigma < 0, \qquad \lambda_3 = 2\sigma > 0 \quad (\sigma > 0).$$
   Here $\dim(\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{comp}}) = 2$, but $\lambda_3 = 2\sigma$ is strongly extensional.
3. **General Triaxial Hyperbolic**:
   $$\lambda_1 < 0, \qquad \lambda_2 \in (-\lambda_1, 0) \text{ or } (0, -\lambda_1), \qquad \lambda_3 > 0.$$

### 2.2 The Geometric Dilemma
In the OpenAI singular core (Section 7), the background flow along the central filament is an axisymmetric stagnation flow with **one** compressive axis (along the filament axis $z$, $\lambda_z = -2\sigma$) and **two** radial extensional axes ($\lambda_r = \lambda_\theta = \sigma > 0$).

Therefore:
$$\boxed{\dim(\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{comp}}) = 1}.$$

**Theorem 2.1 (Two-Family Directional Exclusion)**:
Since $\dim(\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{comp}}) = 1$, the compressive subspace is a single 1D line $\mathbb{R} e_1$.
Because the two stress-generating wavevectors must be linearly independent ($k_+^\star \nparallel k_-^\star$), at most **one** of the two wavevectors can lie in $\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{comp}}$.
The other wavevector, say $k_-^\star$, necessarily has a non-zero projection onto the extensional subspace:
$$\Pi_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{ext}}}(k_-^\star) \ne 0.$$
Consequently, by Theorem 4.1 of `NS_OPENAI_B1_REASSESSMENT_KELVIN_AND_HADAMARD.md`, the second wave family $W_-^\star$ suffers immediate super-exponential backward Hadamard explosion:
$$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{back}}(k_-^\star) \ge \nu |\Pi_{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{ext}}}(k_-^\star)|^2 2^{-j(1+h)} \exp(2 \cdot 2^{jh}) \to +\infty.$$

Even if one family could be de-sheared backward, the **pair** $(W_+^\star, W_-^\star)$ can never be simultaneously generated by the same unforced background strain!

## 3. The Relative Phase Constraint

Even if both wave amplitudes were magically reachable, the Reynolds stress tensor generated by the superposition $w = w_+ + w_-$ contains cross-terms:
$$w \otimes w = w_+ \otimes w_+ + w_- \otimes w_- + \left( w_+ \otimes w_- + w_- \otimes w_+ \right).$$
The cross-term oscillates at the beat frequencies $k_+ \pm k_-$.
Averaging over the fast scale requires the relative phase:
$$\Delta \phi(t) = \phi_+(t) - \phi_-(t) = \Delta \phi(0) + \int_0^t (k_+(s) - k_-(s)) \cdot u_B(X(s), s) ds$$
to land inside an admissible angular window $\Delta \phi(t_j) \in [\Phi^\star - \delta_K, \Phi^\star + \delta_K]$.

Because the accumulated phase difference:
$$\int_0^{t_j} |k_+ - k_-| |u_B| ds \asymp 2^{jh} \gg 1$$
diverges with $j$, any infinitesimal backward initial phase uncertainty $\delta \phi_0 \sim 2^{-jh}$ scrambles the relative orientation at $t_j$, destroying the stress cone landing.

## 4. The Leray Projected Equation

The linearized evolution of the pulse perturbation $w$ under the full incompressible Navier–Stokes operator with Leray projection $\mathbb{P} = I - \nabla(-\Delta)^{-1} \operatorname{div}$ is:
$$\partial_t w + \mathbb{P} \left[ (u_B \cdot \nabla) w + (w \cdot \nabla) u_B \right] - \nu \Delta w = 0.$$

For affine flow $u_B = A(t) x$, $(w \cdot \nabla) u_B = A(t) w$.
The pressure solves $-\Delta p = \operatorname{div}((u_B \cdot \nabla)w + A(t) w) = 2 \operatorname{tr}(A \nabla w)$.
In Fourier space:
$$\widehat{\nabla p}(\xi) = 2 \frac{\xi \xi^T}{|\xi|^2} A(t) \widehat{w}(\xi).$$
The Leray projection maintains $\xi \cdot \widehat{w}(\xi) = 0$ exactly.
Crucially, the Leray projector is a zeroth-order Calderón–Zygmund operator that is bounded on $L^p$ for $1 < p < \infty$, but it **does not alter the parabolic dissipative symbol** $\nu |\xi|^2$.
Therefore, the Leray projection cannot mitigate or regularize the backward Hadamard heat explosion $\exp(+\nu \int |\xi|^2 dt)$; the ill-posedness of the backward parabolic equation is identical with or without the Leray projection.

## 5. Single-Pulse Nonlinear Correction and Contraction Gate Failure

Now consider the full nonlinear unforced Navier–Stokes solution:
$$u = u_B + w + v,$$
where $u_B$ is the background flow, $w$ is the linearized wavepacket, and $v$ is the nonlinear correction field.

### 5.1 The Nonlinear Correction Equation
Substituting $u = u_B + w + v$ into $\partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla)u + \nabla p - \nu \Delta u = 0$ and subtracting the background and linearized equations yields:
$$\partial_t v + \mathbb{P} \left[ (u_B + w) \cdot \nabla v + (v \cdot \nabla)(u_B + w) \right] - \nu \Delta v = -\mathbb{P} \left[ (w \cdot \nabla)w + (v \cdot \nabla)v \right].$$
with zero initial correction: $v(0) = 0$.

### 5.2 The Fixed-Point Contraction Mapping
We seek a solution $v$ in a Banach space $X = C([0, \Delta t_j]; H_\sigma^s(\mathbb{R}^3))$ via the Duhamel fixed-point operator:
$$v = \mathcal{T}(v) = -\int_0^t \mathcal{S}(t, s) \mathbb{P} \left[ (w \cdot \nabla)w + (v \cdot \nabla)v \right](s) ds,$$
where $\mathcal{S}(t, s)$ is the propagator generated by the linearized operator $\mathcal{L} = \partial_t + \mathbb{P}[(u_B + w) \cdot \nabla + (\cdot \nabla)(u_B + w)] - \nu \Delta$.

### 5.3 Contraction Estimate Failure
To establish existence and smallness of $v$, the operator $\mathcal{T}$ must satisfy a contraction estimate:
$$\|\mathcal{T}(v) - \mathcal{T}(\tilde{v})\|_X \le q \|v - \tilde{v}\|_X, \qquad q < 1.$$

Evaluate the Lipschitz factor $q$ over the burst interval $[0, \Delta t_j]$ of duration $\Delta t_j = 2^{-j}$:
The linearized background-pulse advection and stretching operators have norm:
$$\|u_B\|_{Lip} \asymp \sigma(t) \asymp 2^{j(1+h)}, \qquad \|w\|_{Lip} \asymp |k^\star| A^\star \asymp 2^{j/2} \cdot 2^{j(1/2+h)} = 2^{j(1+h)}.$$

Over the time duration $\Delta t_j = 2^{-j}$, the Duhamel operator accumulates a stretching factor:
$$q \approx \int_0^{\Delta t_j} \left( \|u_B\|_{Lip} + \|w\|_{Lip} \right) ds \asymp 2^{j(1+h)} \cdot 2^{-j} = 2^{jh}.$$

For any $h > 0$:
$$\boxed{q \asymp 2^{jh} \gg 1 \quad \text{as } j \to \infty}.$$
The Lipschitz constant $q$ diverges as $2^{jh} \to \infty$.

**Theorem 5.1 (Nonlinear Contraction Gate Failure)**:
The unforced nonlinear perturbation equation for $v$ fails the Picard–Banach contraction theorem in every scale-critical Sobolev space $H^s$. The linearized pulse amplitude $A^\star \asymp 2^{j(1/2+h)}$ is supercritical relative to the unforced viscous timescale, and nonlinear backreaction is of order:
$$\|v(\Delta t_j)\|_{L^2} \gtrsim \|W_j^\star\|_{L^2} \cdot 2^{jh} \gg \|W_j^\star\|_{L^2}.$$
The nonlinear error completely swamps the pulse state; no unforced small-error contraction exists.
