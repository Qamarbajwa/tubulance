## 1. The Annular Target Stress Tensor

When the inner self-similar concentrating profile $u^{\text{in}}$ is matched to the outer heat flow $u^{\text{out}}$, the Navier–Stokes operator generates a momentum residual in the matching transition annulus $r \sim \ell_r(\tau)$:
$$R(u, p) = \partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla)u - \nu \Delta u + \nabla p.$$

To prevent this residual from producing an $\mathcal{O}(\tau^{-3/2 - h})$ blowup away from the axis, the fluid must generate an internal Reynolds stress tensor $T(r, z, t)$ such that:
$$\operatorname{div} T = R(u, p).$$

Because the flow is axisymmetric with swirl, the primary non-vanishing tangential components of this required annular stress reside in the $2$-dimensional subspace spanned by the azimuthal and axial momentum fluxes:
$$T = \begin{pmatrix}
T_{\theta\theta} & T_{\theta z} \\
T_{z\theta} & T_{zz}
\end{pmatrix} \in \operatorname{Sym}^+(2).$$

## 2. Realization via Two Wave Families

A single wave family $w_1 = a_1 \zeta_1 \cos(\Phi_1)$ can only produce a rank-1 Reynolds stress:
$$\langle w_1 \otimes w_1 \rangle = \frac{1}{2} a_1^2 (\zeta_1 \otimes \zeta_1).$$
A rank-1 stress cannot span the 2D or 3D stress space required to match an arbitrary residual $T$.

### 2.1 The Two Wave Vectors
The OpenAI proof introduces **two distinct wave families**, denoted $w^{(1)}$ and $w^{(2)}$, characterized by non-collinear polarization/flux vectors:
$$v_1 = \zeta_1 \otimes \zeta_1, \qquad v_2 = \zeta_2 \otimes \zeta_2.$$

The combined time-integrated Reynolds stress generated by these two wave families is:
$$\langle w \otimes w \rangle = \frac{1}{2} a_1^2 v_1 + \frac{1}{2} a_2^2 v_2.$$

### 2.2 The Stress Cone Representation
Defining coefficients:
$$c_1 = \frac{1}{2} a_1^2 \ge 0, \qquad c_2 = \frac{1}{2} a_2^2 \ge 0,$$
the target stress $T$ is decomposed into:
$$T = c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2.$$

For this decomposition to be physically realizable with real wave amplitudes $a_1, a_2 \in \mathbb{R}$, the coefficients $c_1$ and $c_2$ must be **strictly positive**:
$$c_1 > 0, \qquad c_2 > 0.$$

## 3. The Admissible and Relaxed Stress Cones

### 3.1 Definition of the Admissible Cone
Define the open convex cone $\mathcal{K} \subset \operatorname{Sym}^+(2)$ spanned by the positive linear combinations of $v_1$ and $v_2$:
$$\mathcal{K} \equiv \operatorname{Cone}(v_1, v_2) = \left\{ c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2 \;\Big|\; c_1 > 0, \, c_2 > 0 \right\}.$$
- **Admissibility Condition**: The target residual stress $T$ is *admissible* if and only if:
  $$T \in \mathcal{K}.$$
- If $T \in \mathcal{K}$, the amplitudes of the two wave families are given explicitly by:
  $$a_1 = \sqrt{2 c_1}, \qquad a_2 = \sqrt{2 c_2}.$$

### 3.2 The Relaxed Cone and Geometric Margin
In actual practice, the target stress $T(r, z, t)$ varies across the annulus. To ensure robustness against higher-order perturbations, OpenAI constructs a *relaxed cone* $\mathcal{K}_{\text{relaxed}}$ with a uniform angular opening margin $\delta > 0$:
$$\operatorname{dist}(T, \partial \mathcal{K}) \ge \delta \|T\|.$$
This geometric margin ensures that when higher-order corrective waves are added during the iterative expansion, the perturbed stress does not escape the cone of positive amplitudes.

## 4. Comparison: OpenAI Stress Cone vs. Convex Integration (De Lellis–Székelyhidi / Buckmaster–Vicol)

It is vital to contrast this mechanism with classical convex integration:

$$\begin{array}{|l|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{Convex Integration (Nash / De Lellis–Székelyhidi)} & \textbf{OpenAI Navier–Stokes Blowup Construction} \\
\hline
\textbf{Target Regularity} & C^\alpha \text{ with } \alpha < 1/3 \text{ (Onsager threshold)} & \mathbf{C^\infty \text{ smooth everywhere away from singularity}} \\
\textbf{Wave Nature} & Dense, space-filling Mikado / Beltrami flows & \textbf{Localized wavepackets in narrow annular rings} \\
\textbf{Viscosity} & Handled by high-frequency cutoff; limits $\alpha < 1/3$ & \textbf{Balanced by background shear amplification} \\
\textbf{Stress Metric} & Global Reynolds stress relaxation in $L^1 / L^\infty$ & \textbf{Local cone decomposition } T = c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2 \\
\textbf{Equation Solved} & Non-unique weak solutions (Euler / forced NS) & \textbf{Smooth classical solution with } T_* < \infty \\
\textbf{Forcing Role} & Often unforced for weak Euler; forced for weak NS & \textbf{Smooth forcing } f \in C^\infty \text{ absorbs flat residual} \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

### Mathematical Insight
The OpenAI construction is **not** a standard convex integration scheme. It does not iterate indefinitely in space to produce a fractal, nowhere-differentiable velocity field. Instead, it is an **asymptotic matching and boundary layer control scheme**: it uses two localized oscillatory wave families to cancel a finite-order momentum deficit between an exact inner core and an exact outer heat flow, leaving a smooth remainder that is flat at the singular point.
