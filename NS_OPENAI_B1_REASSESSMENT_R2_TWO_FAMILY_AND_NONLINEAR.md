## 1. Two-Family Pulse Structure Audit (Section 9)

### 1.1 OpenAI Construction Review

From the OpenAI Section 7 construction (as audited in `NS_OPENAI_B1_R1_PULSE_MODEL_AUDIT.md`):

- Two helical branches $b_+, b_-$ with **the same wavevector** $k_j^\star e_z$ (collinear, both along the $z$-axis).
- Opposite circular polarizations: $b_\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_r \pm i e_\theta)$.
- Spatial envelope $\chi_j(r, z)$ localized in the annular filament.

### 1.2 Evolved Covariance Matrices

For a single wave family propagated through the strain:
$$W_\pm(x, t) = A_\pm(t) \chi_j(r, z; t) e^{ik_j(t) \cdot x} b_\pm + \text{c.c.}$$

The stress tensor from each family is:
$$\tau_{ij}^{(\pm)} = \overline{W_\pm^i W_\pm^j} = |A_\pm|^2 |\chi_j|^2 (b_\pm^i \overline{b_\pm^j} + \overline{b_\pm^i} b_\pm^j).$$

For $b_+ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_r + ie_\theta)$:
$$b_+^i \overline{b_+^j} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -i & 0 \\ i & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{(in $(r, \theta, z)$ basis)}.$$

The **real symmetric** stress contribution:
$$\tau^{(+)} = |A_+|^2 |\chi_j|^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}(b_+ \otimes \overline{b_+} + \overline{b_+} \otimes b_+) = \frac{|A_+|^2 |\chi_j|^2}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Similarly for $b_- = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_r - ie_\theta)$:
$$\tau^{(-)} = \frac{|A_-|^2 |\chi_j|^2}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

### 1.3 Key Observation: Identical Covariance Structure

Both $\tau^{(+)}$ and $\tau^{(-)}$ have the **same** real symmetric stress tensor up to amplitude:
$$\tau^{(\pm)} \propto \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Opposite circular polarizations do NOT produce different real covariance structures. They span a **one-dimensional** subspace of symmetric stress space, not a two-dimensional one.

### 1.4 Impact on CLM-037-B

CLM-037-B assumes the coupling matrix $H = [C(b_+) \; C(b_-)]$ is invertible ($\det H \neq 0$). But:
- Since $C(b_+) = C(b_-)$ (up to scalar), $H$ has **rank 1**, not rank 2.
- The claim that "two distinct helical wave families form an invertible 2×2 coupling matrix" is **incorrect for collinear opposite-polarization families sharing the same wavevector**.

However, CLM-037-B also states that under $\dim(E_{\mathrm{comp}}) = 1$, only one wavevector can be de-sheared backward. This part is correct. The rank-1 stress structure merely strengthens the obstruction:
- Even with two families, only one stress direction is accessible.
- The backward explosion applies equally to both families since they share the same wavevector.

### 1.5 CLM-037-B Repair

The corrected statement should be:

> Under the OpenAI construction with collinear wavevectors $k_j^\star e_z$ and opposite circular polarizations $b_\pm$, the two families produce **identical** real symmetric stress tensors (rank-1 stress subspace). The coupling matrix $H = [C(b_+) \; C(b_-)]$ has rank 1, not rank 2. Generating an arbitrary target stress $T^\star$ with $T^\star_{zz} \neq 0$ or off-diagonal components requires additional wave families with non-collinear wavevectors, which would then require backward de-shearing along non-compressive directions, encountering the backward explosion. The obstruction is **strengthened**, not weakened.

## 2. Nonlinear Cross-Interaction Audit (Section 10)

### 2.1 What R1 Did

The R1 nonlinear analysis (`NS_OPENAI_B1_REASSESSMENT_R1_NONLINEAR_CLAIM_REPAIR.md`) treated the nonlinear term as a Lipschitz perturbation with stretching factor $q \asymp 2^{jh} \gg 1$ and argued that the contraction mapping fails.

This is a **dimensional estimate** that does not account for:
1. Zero-frequency (mean) terms generated by quadratic self-interaction.
2. Sum-frequency and difference-frequency terms from cross-interaction of the two families.
3. The spatial envelope structure and its interaction with Leray projection.

### 2.2 Frequency Analysis of Cross Terms

Consider two wave families:
$$W_+ = A_+ \chi_j e^{ik_j z} b_+, \qquad W_- = A_- \chi_j e^{ik_j z} b_-.$$

The quadratic nonlinearity $(W \cdot \nabla)W$ generates:

#### Self-interaction of $W_+$:
$$W_+ \cdot \nabla W_+ \sim |A_+|^2 |\chi_j|^2 e^{2ik_j z} (b_+ \cdot \nabla)b_+ + \text{zero-frequency terms}.$$

The $e^{2ik_j z}$ term is at **double frequency** (sum frequency).
The zero-frequency term comes from the conjugate pair: $\overline{W_+} \cdot \nabla W_+$.

#### Cross-interaction $W_+ \times W_-$ (same wavevector!):
Since both have $e^{ik_j z}$:
$$W_+ \cdot \nabla W_- \sim A_+ \overline{A_-} |\chi_j|^2 e^{2ik_j z} (b_+ \cdot \nabla)b_- + \text{zero-frequency}.$$

Again: sum frequency $2k_j$ and zero frequency.

**No difference-frequency terms** appear because both waves have the same $k_j$, so $k_+ - k_- = 0$ (zero frequency).

### 2.3 Spatial Envelope Effects

The spatial derivatives of $\chi_j$ contribute at order $|\nabla \chi_j| \asymp \chi_j / \delta r_j \asymp \chi_j \cdot 2^{j/2}$.

The full expansion:
$$(W_+ \cdot \nabla)W_- = A_+ A_- [\chi_j^2 e^{2ik_j z} (b_+ \cdot ik_j e_z)b_- + \chi_j (b_+ \cdot \nabla \chi_j) e^{2ik_j z} b_- + \text{conjugate terms}].$$

The envelope gradient term has magnitude:
$$|\chi_j (b_+ \cdot \nabla \chi_j)| \asymp |\chi_j|^2 / \delta r_j \asymp |\chi_j|^2 \cdot 2^{j/2}.$$

Compared to the carrier gradient term:
$$|\chi_j^2 k_j| \asymp |\chi_j|^2 \cdot 2^{j/2}.$$

They are the **same order**. The envelope gradient is not negligible.

### 2.4 Leray Projection

The Leray projection $\mathbb{P}$ acts on the nonlinear term to enforce divergence-free output. For oscillatory terms $\sim e^{2ik_j z}$, the projection modifies amplitude but not the oscillatory structure:
$$\mathbb{P}[f \cdot e^{2ik_j z}] = \left(I - \frac{2k_j e_z \otimes 2k_j e_z}{|2k_j|^2}\right) f \cdot e^{2ik_j z} + \text{corrections from } \nabla f.$$

The key point: the projection kills the $e_z$ component of the double-frequency term but preserves the transverse $(e_r, e_\theta)$ components.

### 2.5 Impact Assessment

The nonlinear cross-interaction audit reveals:

1. **Zero-frequency terms**: These contribute to the mean flow and modify the background. They are at most order $|A_j|^2 |\chi_j|^2 \asymp 2^{j(1+2h)} |\chi_j|^2$, which is the same order as the target stress. The mean flow modification could in principle be constructive or destructive.

2. **Sum-frequency terms**: At $2k_j$, these are higher-frequency oscillations. Under forward propagation, they are exponentially damped by viscosity at rate $\nu |2k_j|^2 \asymp 4\nu \cdot 2^j$. They are transient.

3. **Envelope gradient terms**: Same order as carrier gradient terms. Cannot be neglected in the exact nonlinear analysis.

4. **Bottom line**: The R1 Lipschitz estimate $q \asymp 2^{jh}$ captures the correct order of magnitude for the nonlinear stretching. A full nonlinear analysis would need to track the zero-frequency mean flow feedback and the envelope gradient contributions. However, the **backward cost divergence** (which is the core of CLM-038) is a property of the **linearized** operator and is not affected by the nonlinear terms, which enter only at higher order in the perturbation expansion.
