## 1. The Five Preload and Precursor Mechanisms (P0–P4)

In `NS-EXT-OPENAI-001`, the preloading route was evaluated under the restrictive assumption that each high-frequency wavepacket $w_j$ is present in initial data with its final active wavenumber $|k_j| \sim 2^{j/2}$ throughout the entire time interval $t \in [0, t_j]$.

Under rigorous mathematical scrutiny, the preloading/pulse-generation problem must be partitioned into **five distinct physical classes**:

| Class ID | Mechanism Name | Description of Initial State at $t = 0$ | Dynamical Evolution up to $t_j$ | Status in R1 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P0** | **Direct Final-Frequency Preload** | Mode $j$ seeded at $t=0$ with final wavenumber $|k_j(0)| \asymp \tau_j^{-1/2} = 2^{j/2}$. | Constant high frequency; subject to unattenuated diffusion $\nu \cdot 2^j$ over time $t_j \approx 1$. | **RIGOROUSLY FALSIFIED** by $\exp(-\nu 2^j)$. |
| **P1** | **Low-Frequency Precursor** | Mode $j$ seeded at $t=0$ with low wavenumber $|k_j(0)| \asymp K_0 = \mathcal{O}(1)$. | Wavenumber remains low until $t \approx t_j - \Delta t_j$, then sheared to $2^{j/2}$ by singular core gradient. | **SURVIVES (VALID PRECURSOR ROUTE)**. |
| **P2** | **Nonlinear Mode Generation** | Seeded by resonant triad interactions between earlier modes $j' < j$. | Generated dynamically at intermediate times without initial presence in $u_0$. | **OPEN / VIABLE**. |
| **P3** | **Turbulent Cascade Generation** | Generated by turbulent energy transfer from macro-scales. | Stochastic / broadband cascade injects energy into ring mode. | **OPEN / HEURISTIC**. |
| **P4** | **Dormant Instability Seed** | Eigenmode of a secondary instability that is linearly stable until core exceeds threshold. | Zero growth until background strain crosses critical bifurcating Rayleigh/Taylor number. | **OPEN / STRUCTURALLY ATTRACTIVE**. |

$$\boxed{\mathbf{RULE:\ A\ proof\ against\ P0\ must\ NOT\ be\ used\ as\ a\ theorem\ against\ P1\text{--}P4.}}$$

## 2. Derivation of the Exact Time-Dependent Damping Integral

Let $w_j(x, t) \approx a_j(t) e^{i k_j(t) \cdot x} \zeta_j(t)$ be a wavepacket evolving in the time-dependent background velocity field $U(x, t)$.

### 2.1 The Ray-Tracing and Amplitude Equations
1. **Wavevector Evolution (Advective Shearing)**:
   $$\frac{d}{dt} k_j(t) = - (\nabla U(X_j(t), t))^T k_j(t).$$
2. **Exact Amplitude Logarithmic Growth**:
   $$\frac{d}{dt} \ln a_j(t) = \Gamma_j(t) - \nu |k_j(t)|^2 + E_j(t),$$
   where:
   - $\Gamma_j(t) = - \frac{\zeta_j^T S(U) \zeta_j}{|\zeta_j|^2}$ is the instantaneous shear amplification rate.
   - $\nu |k_j(t)|^2$ is the **exact instantaneous viscous dissipation rate**.
   - $E_j(t)$ represents nonlinear cross-talk or external forcing ($E_j = 0$ for unforced autonomous evolution).

### 2.2 The Integrated Amplitude Formula
Integrating from $t = 0$ to activation time $t_j$:
$$\boxed{a_j(t_j) = a_j(0) \exp\left[ \int_0^{t_j} \left( \Gamma_j(s) - \nu |k_j(s)|^2 \right) ds \right].}$$

### 2.3 Why P0 Fails (The Static High-Frequency Fallacy)
Under hypothesis P0, $|k_j(s)| \equiv 2^{j/2}$ for all $s \in [0, t_j]$.
Since $t_j = 1 - 2^{-j} \ge 1/2$:
$$\nu \int_0^{t_j} |k_j(s)|^2 ds \ge \nu \cdot 2^j \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\nu}{2} 2^j \implies \text{Damping } \exp\left(-\frac{\nu}{2} 2^j\right).$$
Compensating for this with $a_j(0) \sim \exp(+\frac{\nu}{2} 2^j)$ blows up $\|u_0\|_{H^s}$.
**This falsifies P0, and P0 only.**

## 3. Mathematical Evaluation of the Precursor Hypothesis (P1)

Now consider hypothesis **P1**, where the mode is seeded at a fixed low wavenumber:
$$|k_j(0)| = K_0 \asymp \mathcal{O}(1) \quad (\text{independent of } j).$$

### 3.1 Two-Phase Wavevector Dynamics
The history $[0, t_j]$ partitions into two distinct dynamical regimes:
1. **Dormant Quiescent Phase ($t \in [0, t_j - \Delta t_j]$)**:
   - The singular core has not yet reached scale $j$.
   - The background velocity gradient in the wavepacket region is moderate: $\|\nabla U\| \le C_{\text{macro}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$.
   - The wavevector remains bounded:
     $$|k_j(s)| \le K_0 e^{C_{\text{macro}} s} \le K_0 e^{C_{\text{macro}}} \equiv K_{\max} = \mathcal{O}(1).$$
   - **Integrated Dissipation in Phase 1**:
     $$\nu \int_0^{t_j - \Delta t_j} |k_j(s)|^2 ds \le \nu K_{\max}^2 \cdot 1 = \mathcal{O}(1) \quad (\textbf{Uniformly Bounded in } j!).$$

2. **Active Shearing Phase ($t \in [t_j - \Delta t_j, t_j]$)**:
   - The duration of this active window matches the local self-similar timescale:
     $$\Delta t_j \asymp \tau_j = 2^{-j}.$$
   - During this burst, the wavepacket enters the singular shear layer, where the strain rate is:
     $$\|S(U)\| \asymp \tau_j^{-1 - h} = 2^{j(1 + h)}.$$
   - Over this short interval $\Delta t_j$, the wavevector is sheared from $K_{\max} \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ to its active value $|k_j(t_j)| \asymp \tau_j^{-1/2} = 2^{j/2}$.
   - **Integrated Dissipation in Phase 2**:
     $$\nu \int_{t_j - \Delta t_j}^{t_j} |k_j(s)|^2 ds \le \nu (2^{j/2})^2 \cdot \Delta t_j = \nu 2^j \cdot 2^{-j} = \nu = \mathcal{O}(1) \quad (\textbf{Uniformly Bounded in } j!).$$

### 3.2 The Total Viscous Damping Exponent
Combining both phases:
$$\boxed{\nu \int_0^{t_j} |k_j(s)|^2 ds \le \nu K_{\max}^2 + \nu = \mathcal{O}(1) \quad \text{uniformly for all } j \ge 1!}$$
The total viscous damping experienced by a P1 precursor is:
$$\exp\left(-\nu \int_0^{t_j} |k_j(s)|^2 ds\right) \ge \exp(-\mathcal{O}(1)) \ge c_0 > 0.$$
**Viscous dissipation does NOT extinguish the precursor!**

## 4. The Net Amplification Integral and Initial Data Smoothness

Now calculate the shear amplification integral over the active burst:
$$\int_{t_j - \Delta t_j}^{t_j} \Gamma_j(s) ds \asymp \sigma_1(t_j) \cdot \Delta t_j \asymp 2^{j(1 + h)} \cdot 2^{-j} = 2^{jh} \xrightarrow[j \to \infty]{} +\infty.$$

### 4.1 Net Amplification Factor
Since $h > 0$ (e.g. $h = 0.05$):
$$\int_0^{t_j} \left( \Gamma_j(s) - \nu |k_j(s)|^2 \right) ds \ge C_1 2^{jh} - C_2 \xrightarrow[j \to \infty]{} +\infty.$$
The fluid provides **divergent net exponential amplification** to the precursor!

### 4.2 Required Initial Amplitude $a_j(0)$
To produce a target pulse amplitude $a_j(t_j) = \mathcal{O}(1)$ required by the stress cone:
$$a_j(0) = a_j(t_j) \exp\left[ - \int_0^{t_j} (\Gamma_j - \nu |k_j|^2) ds \right] \le C \exp\left( - C_1 2^{jh} \right).$$

### 4.3 Initial Velocity Regularity Audit
Construct the initial velocity field:
$$u_0(x) = u_{0, \text{base}}(x) + \sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(0) w_j(x, 0).$$
Let us check the Sobolev norm $\|u_0\|_{H^m}$ for any $m \ge 0$:
$$\|u_0\|_{H^m} \le \|u_{0, \text{base}}\|_{H^m} + \sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(0) |k_j(0)|^m \le \|u_{0, \text{base}}\|_{H^m} + K_0^m \sum_{j=1}^\infty C \exp\left( - C_1 2^{jh} \right).$$
Since $2^{jh} \gg j$, the series $\sum_{j=1}^\infty \exp(-C_1 2^{jh})$ converges with astronomical speed!
- **Smoothness**: $u_0 \in C^\infty(\mathbb{T}^3)$ (in fact, real-analytic $C^\omega$).
- **Incompressibility**: By choosing $w_j(x, 0) = \nabla \times \psi_j(x)$, $\nabla \cdot u_0 = 0$ holds identically.
- **Energy**: $\|u_0\|_{L^2} < \infty$.
