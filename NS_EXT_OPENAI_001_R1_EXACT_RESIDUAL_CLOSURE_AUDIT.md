## 1. Problem Formulation: The Exact Residual Closure Problem

Let $(u, p)$ be an approximate blowup solution constructed by asymptotic matching, satisfying the Navier–Stokes momentum equation with a smooth remainder:
$$R(u, p) \equiv \partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla)u - \nu \Delta u + \nabla p = f_{\text{smooth}}, \qquad \nabla \cdot u = 0.$$

In the OpenAI proof, $f_{\text{smooth}}$ is assigned to be the external body force, proving forced breakdown.
For Clay Statement B on $\mathbb{T}^3$, we require:
$$R(u + v, \, p + q) \equiv 0, \qquad \nabla \cdot v = 0.$$

We formally denote this the:
$$\boxed{\mathbf{EXACT\_RESIDUAL\_CLOSURE\_PROBLEM}}$$

### Correction of Predecessor Equivalence Claim
In `NS-EXT-OPENAI-001`, it was stated that solving this problem is *"logically equivalent to solving the entire Clay problem"*.
This statement is mathematically imprecise and unproved. The exact residual closure problem is a **local-in-time perturbation problem around a specific, highly structured self-similar background profile $u$ with an exponentially flat source $R \sim \exp(-1/\tau)$**. This is a well-defined parabolic PDE perturbation problem, distinct from the generic Cauchy problem for arbitrary initial data.

## 2. Derivation of the Linearized Operator $\mathcal{L}_u$

Let $u_{\text{exact}} = u + v$ and $p_{\text{exact}} = p + q$.
Substituting into the Navier–Stokes equations:
$$\partial_t(u + v) + ((u + v)\cdot\nabla)(u + v) - \nu \Delta(u + v) + \nabla(p + q) = 0.$$

Subtracting the background residual $R(u, p)$:
$$\mathcal{L}_u v + \nabla q = - R(u, p) - \mathcal{N}(v),$$
where:
1. **The Linearized Operator**:
   $$\mathcal{L}_u v \equiv \partial_t v + (u \cdot \nabla)v + (v \cdot \nabla)u - \nu \Delta v.$$
2. **The Quadratic Remainder**:
   $$\mathcal{N}(v) \equiv (v \cdot \nabla)v.$$
3. **The Incompressibility Constraint**:
   $$\nabla \cdot v = 0.$$

## 3. Quantitative Evaluation of Solution Frameworks

To solve $\mathcal{L}_u v = -R - \mathcal{N}(v)$ with $v \in C^\infty(\mathbb{T}^3 \times [0, 1))$ such that $u + v$ remains smooth up to $t < 1$ and preserves the blowup at $t = 1$, we evaluate five analytical frameworks:

### 3.1 Contraction Mapping in Anisotropic Parabolic Hölder / Sobolev Spaces
- The background velocity satisfies $\|u(\cdot, t)\|_{L^\infty} \asymp \tau^{-1/2 - h}$ and $\|\nabla u(\cdot, t)\|_{L^\infty} \asymp \tau^{-1 - h}$.
- The stretching term $(v \cdot \nabla)u$ introduces a coefficient of size $\tau^{-1 - h}$.
- By Duhamel's formula:
  $$v(t) = - \int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta} \mathbb{P} \left[ (u \cdot \nabla)v + (v \cdot \nabla)u + (v \cdot \nabla)v + R \right](s) ds.$$
- The Grönwall factor generated by $\|\nabla u\|$ is:
  $$\exp\left( \int_0^t \|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty} ds \right) \asymp \exp\left( \int_0^t (1 - s)^{-1 - h} ds \right) = \exp\left( \frac{1}{h \tau^h} \right).$$
- **The Source-Growth Race**:
  The flat residual satisfies $\|R(\cdot, s)\|_{L^\infty} \le C_M \tau^M$ for any $M \in \mathbb{N}$, and typically $\|R\| \sim \exp(-c/\tau)$.
  Comparing the exponential growth of the propagator with the decay of the source:
  $$\|v(t)\| \le \exp\left( \frac{1}{h \tau^h} \right) \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{c}{\tau} \right) = \exp\left( -\frac{c}{\tau} + \frac{1}{h \tau^h} \right).$$
  Since $h < 1$, as $\tau \to 0^+$:
  $$\frac{c}{\tau} \gg \frac{1}{h \tau^h} \implies -\frac{c}{\tau} + \frac{1}{h \tau^h} \xrightarrow[\tau \to 0^+]{} -\infty!$$
- **Quantitative Finding**: The exponential flatness of $R$ **overwhelms the Grönwall growth of the linearized operator in $L^\infty$**!
- **The Derivative Loss Barrier**: However, in estimating $\nabla v$, the pressure projection $\mathbb{P} \nabla(v \otimes v)$ and $(u \cdot \nabla)v$ require spatial derivatives. In standard Sobolev spaces $H^s$, each spatial derivative gains a factor of $\ell_r^{-1} \sim \tau^{-1/2}$, leading to an infinite loss of derivatives unless analytic/Gevrey norms are used.

### 3.2 Formal Series Classification: Gevrey Class vs. Factorial Divergence
- In `NS-EXT-OPENAI-001`, it was asserted without proof that the expansion has "factorial divergence" and "failed Borel summation".
- **Quantitative Correction**:
  Let the expansion be written in powers of the self-similar scale parameter $q = \tau^{1/2}$:
  $$u \sim \sum_{n=0}^\infty u_n(x/\tau^{1/2}) q^n.$$
  The coefficient growth $\|u_n\|$ is determined by the quadratic convolution $\sum_{k=0}^n u_k \nabla u_{n-k}$ and the diffusion operator $\Delta$.
  In Burgers- and Navier–Stokes-type boundary layer matchings, such series are known to belong to **Gevrey class $s = 2$**:
  $$\|u_n\| \le C^n (n!)^2.$$
- **Classification Status**:
  $$\boxed{\mathbf{GEVREY\ (s = 2)\ /\ BOREL\ SUMMABILITY\ UNPROVED\ BUT\ NOT\ EXCLUDED.}}$$
  The formal series cannot be trivially certified divergent or Borel summable without explicit calculation of the Gevrey index and Stokes multipliers on the Riemann sheet of $\tau$.

## 4. Internal Nonlinear Stress Closure ($R = -\nabla \cdot Q$)

A distinct alternative to inverting $\mathcal{L}_u$ is to represent the residual $R$ as the divergence of internal nonlinear Reynolds stresses generated by additional wave families:
$$R = - \operatorname{div} Q_{\text{internal}}.$$

### 4.1 Dimensional Degrees of Freedom in 3D
- The residual vector field $R(r, z, t)$ has 3 components.
- Incompressibility $\nabla \cdot R = 0$ leaves **2 independent solenoidal degrees of freedom**.
- A general symmetric stress tensor $Q \in \operatorname{Sym}(3)$ has **6 independent components**.
- The existing OpenAI construction uses only a $2 \times 2$ sub-block $T \in \operatorname{Sym}^+(2)$ in the $(\theta, z)$ coordinates, spanned by two wave families $v_1, v_2$.

### 4.2 Candidate Internal Closure Modes
1. **Non-Axisymmetric Modes ($m \ge 1$)**:
   The existing proof restricts background waves to axisymmetric rings ($m = 0$). Adding non-axisymmetric helical modes ($e^{i(m\theta + k_z z)}$) introduces off-diagonal stresses $Q_{r\theta}$ and $Q_{rz}$ that directly balance radial and helical residual components.
2. **Cross-Scale Triad Interactions ($Q_{\text{triad}}$)**:
   Interactions between the core scale $\tau^{1/2}$ and the precursor scale $\mathcal{O}(1)$ generate cross-stress $\langle u_{\text{core}} \otimes w_{\text{precursor}} \rangle$ that can absorb annular residual momentum without external body forces.
