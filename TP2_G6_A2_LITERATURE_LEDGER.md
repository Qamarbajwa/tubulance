### 3. Detailed Audit of Key Transfer Theorems

#### 3.1 Constantin–Iyer Representation (LIT-B2-01)
The Constantin–Iyer formulation expresses the solution to the 3D Navier–Stokes equations as:
$$
u(x, t) = \mathbb{P}_{\text{Leray}} \mathbb{E}\left[ (\nabla A_t(x))^T u_0(A_t(x)) \right].
$$
- **Validity Range**: For any classical solution on $[0, T^*)$, the formula is exact.
- **Deformation Tensor Evolution**:
  $$
  d(\nabla A_s) = - (\nabla u(A_s, s)) (\nabla A_s) ds + \text{stochastic terms in back-to-front formulation}.
  $$
  Integrating gives:
  $$
  \|\nabla A_s(x)\| \le \exp\left( \int_s^t \|\nabla u(\cdot, \tau)\|_{L^\infty} d\tau \right).
  $$
- **Obstruction**: Under candidate blowup ($M(t) \to \infty$), $\int_0^{T^*} \|\nabla u\|_{L^\infty} d\tau = \infty$. Thus, $\|\nabla A_t(x)\|$ can blow up pathwise, and the expectation $\mathbb{E}[(\nabla A_t)^T u_0(A_t)]$ is not bounded by any a priori energy quantity.
- **Verdict**: Cannot be used across $T^*$ without an independent bound on $\nabla u$, which would be circular.

#### 3.2 Aronson Heat Kernel Bounds (LIT-B2-02)
Aronson's theorem guarantees two-sided Gaussian bounds for the transition density of the diffusion operator $\partial_t - \nu \Delta + u \cdot \nabla$ provided the drift satisfies:
$$
u \in L^q_t L^p_x, \qquad \frac{2}{q} + \frac{d}{p} < 1.
$$
- **Navier–Stokes Scaling**: In $d=3$, the energy bound gives:
  $$
  u \in L^\infty_t L^2_x \cap L^2_t H^1_x \hookrightarrow L^2_t L^6_x.
  $$
- **Serrin Index Evaluation**:
  $$
  \frac{2}{q} + \frac{3}{p} = \frac{2}{2} + \frac{3}{6} = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} > 1.
  $$
- **Deficit**: The energy bounds miss the Aronson condition by a scaling gap of $\Delta = 1/2$.
- **Consequence**: Counterexamples exist (see WP5) where convective sweeping concentrates the probability mass outside the high-vorticity region, causing the transition density $p(s, y; t, x)$ to vanish faster than any Gaussian lower bound in the direction opposite to the drift.

### 4. Epistemic Conclusion on Literature Transfer

The literature audit formally establishes:
1. **No Existing Regularity Proof via Single SDEs**: No result in the published mathematical literature proves global regularity of 3D Navier–Stokes via single-particle stochastic flows without assuming Ladyzhenskaya–Prodi–Serrin, BKM, or direction coherence.
2. **Deficit is Structural**: The failure of Aronson lower bounds, Girsanov equivalence, and $L^p$ Radon–Nikodym bounds is rooted in the exact same scaling gap: the Navier–Stokes energy inequality is subcritical by $1/2$ derivative in 3D.
3. **Multi-Particle / Stochastic Jet Mandate**: The only way to circumvent the single-particle drift singularity without assuming regularity is to measure the **relative dispersion** of two or more particles:
   $$
   \delta A_s(x, h) = A_s(x+h) - A_s(x),
   $$
   where the mean drift cancels to leading order ($\delta u \approx (\nabla u) h$), transforming the problem into a scale-invariant covariance problem (Bridge Target G6-C).
