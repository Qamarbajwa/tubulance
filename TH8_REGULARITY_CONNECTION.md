# TH8 REGULARITY CONNECTION: WHY DEPLETION DOES NOT YET PROVE REGULARITY

## 1. Pointwise Growth Bound on Spatial Maximum
At $\mathbf{x}^*(t) \in \operatorname{argmax} |\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{x}, t)|$, $\Delta|\boldsymbol{\omega}| \le 0 \implies V_{\log} \le 0$. Thus:
$$\frac{D\ln\|\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\|_{L^\infty}}{Dt} \le \lambda_\omega(\mathbf{x}^*(t), t) \le \lambda_3(\mathbf{x}^*(t), t)$$

## 2. The Unresolved Scaling Obstacle
Even though statistical depletion reduces $\lambda_\omega$ to $\sim 0.38 \lambda_3$ on average, $\lambda_3$ scales as $O(|\boldsymbol{\omega}|)$ in $L^\infty$ via the singular Riesz transform (Biot-Savart law). Because $D_{\text{ratio}}$ can reach $\approx 0.94$ in transient near-extensional states, no universal $\delta > 0$ prevents Riccati-type $O(|\boldsymbol{\omega}|^2)$ growth without an independent global estimate on $\int_0^T \lambda_3 dt$.
